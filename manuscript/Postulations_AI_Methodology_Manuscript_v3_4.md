# Human-Initiated Multi-AI Research Coordination in the Postulations Project

*A retrospective case study of human steering, research specialization, recurring coordination, and evidence exchange across nine papers*

John Giles | Scope-alignment editorial v3.4 | 19 November 2026

Scope: This paper examines the human-initiated organization of nine related research papers, the concentration of additional research on Paper II, cross-paper coordination, external-source and mathematics support, scheduled work and the preservation of decisions through GitHub. Cross-AI review is one component of that existing workflow, illustrated by an earlier UQCP manuscript sequence. The later tools developed to study this workflow—including episode coding, reconstructed transcripts and participant audits—are not presented as original operating practices. The underlying theories, comparative effectiveness and original-chat chronology remain unverified. AI service/model identifiers remain unspecified in this anonymized working draft, pending venue-compliant disclosure.

## Abstract

This retrospective case study examines how one human researcher organized and adapted an AI-assisted scientific research program comprising nine related but separately testable papers. The author assigned paper-specific research conversations, concentrated multiple workstreams on Paper II, established cross-paper coordination and source-intelligence roles, and used GitHub to retain research artifacts and decisions. A November 17, 2026 snapshot documents ten enabled hourly paper-owner tasks; task completion did not establish correct-chat delivery or scientific adoption. Selected paper-owner records show an external-source packet used while a physical-test restriction remained, a Paper II proposal reviewed without adoption, a software-replay failure and repair, and reported routing problems. A separate AI service also critiqued successive UQCP manuscripts before the nine-paper repository system; that episode illustrates author-mediated cross-AI review, not an additional coordinator or an experimental replication. On November 19, the author introduced a mathematics-specialist role after identifying repeated mathematical work without decisive empirical progress; its effectiveness remains untested. Later event codes and critique inventories were developed for this retrospective study, not imposed as the original workflow. The contribution is a source-bounded organizational case history and a framework for evaluating information transfer, human oversight and operational continuity—not evidence that the nine postulations are correct or that this research architecture outperforms alternatives.

## 1 Introduction and research questions

A finished research manuscript rarely shows every discarded proposal, source dispute, failed calculation, or intervention that shaped its claims. This problem becomes harder when AI-assisted work continues over many conversations and scheduled runs. A repository can preserve an artifact without showing whether another conversation received, assessed, or adopted it. Studying this distinction matters because generated text, successful automation, and scientific acceptance are different events. Recent auditing research likewise shows that intermediate execution records can reveal methodological problems that final manuscripts conceal. [R8-R9]

John Giles identifies the original Postulations project as the starting collection. He further identifies two conceptual lineages: the Unified Quantum Consciousness Postulation is the origin of Paper I, while Duality as Infinity is the origin of Papers II-IX. This is author-attributed intellectual ancestry, not a claim that every later result was already contained in either early text. Postulations2 is devoted to Paper II, with additional Paper II conversations in the second-edition project. Paper II is the focus within a nine-paper series whose members cross-reference information while retaining distinct questions. A coordination chat organizes the work through GitHub, and a source-research chat investigates relevant literature and data. [P14, P16-P19, P24-P26]

The underlying research connects information, accessibility, perception, experience, and action or interaction. Its papers are intended to be independently testable. Governing instructions distinguish mathematical deductions, simulations, empirical observations, and speculative extensions; they prohibit treating an unverified claim in one paper as an established premise in another. [P5, P9] This methods paper examines the conduct of that research. Documenting a correction or a well-qualified handoff does not establish the proposed consciousness or physical mechanisms.

The research questions return to the organizational questions posed before the reviewing service's critiques of this methodology manuscript. **RQ1 — Organization:** How did one human researcher divide and coordinate nine scientific paper scopes, including a concentrated multi-chat Paper II subprogram and specialized research support? **RQ2 — Research exchange:** How were scientific proposals, mathematical work, original-source evidence, cross-AI criticism, qualifications and adoption decisions transferred and reconciled across roles? **RQ3 — Operational continuity and limits:** What do the retained records establish about recurring schedules, GitHub persistence, human oversight, successful or failed delivery, and unresolved research-workflow problems? These questions concern the entire research organization. The earlier UQCP review is one illustration within RQ2, not the object of RQ2 itself.

The study reconstructs the research organization as it existed before this methods-paper review cycle, then explicitly marks later adaptations and documentation activities. Four November coordination cases are the core operating examples; one earlier scientific-review loop is an illustrative cross-AI method; and feedback on this manuscript is later participant commentary. The event ledger, 46-item critique inventory, access audit and proposed comparison are supporting analytical apparatus, not a replacement for the original research design.

## 2 Related research and candidate contribution

Several published systems already implement substantial parts of AI-assisted scientific research. Agent Laboratory progresses from a human-supplied idea through literature review, experimentation, and writing with staged human feedback. The Virtual Lab uses an AI principal investigator and specialist agents with human guidance, and reports laboratory validation of designed nanobodies. Co-Scientist organizes biomedical hypothesis generation and evaluation, while The AI Scientist automates parts of the idea-to-paper cycle. The Kosmos preprint reports sustained literature and data-analysis cycles linked to code and citations. The recent DiscoveryAgent preprint explicitly studies scientist-led steering and checkpoint-based audit. Its audit agents interrogate checkpointed investigation traces, whereas our descriptive unit is a distinct recipient's later handling of a transferred claim, including uncertain receipt or adoption; this comparison asserts no advantage over that system. Human input, specialized agents, long-running research, and retained traces are therefore established precedents, not original inventions of the Postulations project. [R1-R5, R10]

A second literature strand concerns what counts as a reliable outcome. An expert-blinded ideation study separates judged novelty from feasibility and cautions against LLM self-evaluation. ScienceAgentBench evaluates agents against scientist-validated computational tasks rather than treating a paper-like output as proof of competence. A published audit of automated AI researchers found that final manuscripts masked some methodological errors visible in fuller logs. OpenDiscoveryTrace, a 2026 preprint, likewise studies process-level trajectories. These results motivate independent evidence assessment, explicit missingness, and preserved intermediate records; their numeric findings are not transferable to this case. [R6-R9]

The distinctive unit is the **research organization**, rather than a new AI model or a single review loop. Nine papers have different scientific responsibilities; Paper II receives a concentration of additional conversations; a coordinator governs interfaces and stopping rules; Source Intelligence collects outside data and contradictory evidence; GitHub retains versioned records; and the author adapts the role structure when problems arise. Cross-AI scientific criticism and later mathematics specialization are additional functions with their own dates and evidence limits. Existing agent systems already use specialization and critical review. This is a documented case of differentiated human-AI work, not a claim that the components are new or that their combination is superior. This targeted comparison is not a systematic literature review.

Robin is another published multi-agent scientific-discovery system, including experimentally guided assessment outside its generative agents. The W3C PROV family supplies a standard vocabulary for agents, activities and entities in provenance records, while the National Academies distinguishes computational reproducibility using the same data and code from replicability with new data. Our proposed claim-recipient log is an applied case coding instrument, not an original provenance formalism. A second AI provider does not by itself guarantee independent information, context or empirical validation. [R11-R13]

The design also intersects established research-method and provenance traditions. Yin’s case-study approach requires clearly bounded units, rival explanations and an inspectable evidence trail; Lincoln and Guba describe audit and reflexive checks in qualitative inquiry. Nanopublications link small assertions to provenance, while micropublications model claims with their supporting evidence and challenges. ACM artifact-evaluation criteria distinguish accessible materials from independently reproduced results. The event log operationalizes selected distinctions for conversational handoffs; it claims no new universal provenance formalism. [R14-R18]

At the later detailed inspection, nine paper tasks had completed latest-run records, while MAIN had a newer run in progress and retained its previous completed P2-465 output. Scheduled runs have distinct nominal phases, but observed execution windows can overlap. Some recorded starts precede nominal scheduling times; those fields are retained without assuming a latency explanation. Available metadata does not establish uninterrupted hourly execution, a complete success rate, or cost. Task completion, correct conversation delivery, repository retention, software verification, scientific review, and manuscript adoption are separately observable outcomes. [P20, P23]

### 4.3 Contribution and decision attribution

The attribution register should distinguish (1) human question and task commissioning, (2) workflow permission, (3) paper-owner scientific assessment, (4) documented claim-level ratification, and (5) human manuscript-version circulation or release. AI research roles include retrieval, drafting, mathematical work, software tests and critique. A byline or repository author field does not establish which individual adopted a particular claim. Early manuscripts do not identify all historical tools or collaborators. [P2, P6, P17, P19]

John’s reported strategic leadership and recorded author instructions support human initiation; the owner registry separately allocates scientific review and approval. Specific non-adoptions in the sampled records cannot be converted into a program-wide proportion of delegated adoption. Separate AI conversations and services may share prompts, documents and prior work, so agreement between them is not independent scientific corroboration. [P16-P17, P19]

John added a dedicated mathematics specialist on 19 November 2026 because paper-specific conversations were reportedly spending repeated iterations on mathematical developments without generating discriminating tests. The role is intended to review all nine papers’ mathematics, challenge derivations, identify relevant prior art, and propose new formulas when justified, while returning scientific adoption decisions to the individual paper owners. Its two-consecutive-math-only-iteration pivot rule ties mathematical exploration back to tests, adversarial models or explicit stopped conclusions. This is an observed organizational adaptation prompted by human judgment; no subsequent benefit or change in error rate has yet been measured. [S-GOV-01]

### 4.4 Cross-AI scientific criticism as one support function

John reports regularly giving research manuscripts to a separate AI reviewing service, apart from the AI conversations developing those papers. The reviewer critiques physical assumptions, mathematical consistency, controls, feasibility and interpretive claims; John returns the criticism to the appropriate writing/research stream and supplies revised drafts for another round. Independence here means separation from the primary drafting role and service. It does not imply a blind reviewer, isolation from shared information, independent experiments, or human peer review. The four UQCP v6-v9 drafts illustrate this process before the November nine-paper repository architecture. The general frequency of reviews across all nine papers remains author-reported rather than counted from a complete archive. [P42-P48, P55-P58]

A later distinction matters: the same reviewing service subsequently critiqued this methodology manuscript and helped correct its own historical descriptions of the UQCP cycle. Those later comments were valuable participant feedback because the service was now part of the process being studied, not an independent outside methods referee. The participant-reconstructed review messages and their corrected provenance are retained in P55 and P58. The evidence supports successive review and visible draft changes, but exact original message times, causal influence on editing, and any scientific benefit remain unknown. [P42-P44, P55-P58]

## 5 Case design and analysis plan

### 5.1 Corpus and inspection scope

The case comprises one nine-paper program, with multiple paper-specific AI research conversations, supporting roles and a human-relayed reviewing channel. Fifty-eight heterogeneous source entries include dated manuscripts, repository records, run snapshots, author instructions, source dossiers and participant-generated review accounts. They are not 58 independent validations. Detailed operating observations cluster in November 2026; one earlier UQCP review-loop example is discussed separately as an illustration of cross-AI scientific review. [P1-P58]

For every material episode, the analysis distinguishes the date a document was created or stored, the date an action was reported to occur, the source version inspected, and the date of later verification. A repository readback establishes retention at a source head, not truth of a calculation; a chat handoff may report an earlier decision without supplying the original conversation. Exact-owner decisions and retained execution records carry more weight than summaries that repeat them, but no internal source is a substitute for independent empirical validation. Conversation exports, historic model configurations, and complete run archives are incomplete. Absence from this corpus is classified as unknown, not as proof an event never occurred.

Three project-specific historian streams submitted six Original Postulations packets, six Dynamic Door historical findings, and thirteen Postulations2 findings across two packets. Some entries rely on the same policy files, PDFs, or coordinator summaries, so they cannot be treated as independent confirmation. The analysis links repeated accounts to one underlying event, records the chain from contemporaneous artifact to later description, and flags source conflicts. The coordinator directly checked a bounded selection of branch-owner registries, handoff decisions, checkpoint records, and retrieval-coverage files. The checked documents confirm their own contents and recorded states, not all earlier conversations or scientific conclusions. [P28-P30]

Direct file bytes and immutable repository decisions are distinguished from later summaries, participant-reproduced text, inferred exposure and missing records. The UQCP source chain consists of four retained PDFs and an account reconstructed by the reviewer from its own context; it is not an authenticated original conversation export. A subsequent 46-item coordinator classification is retained in Supplement S6 as an illustration of what can be coded, not as an estimate of reviewer effectiveness. Historical September file identity and message timing remain unresolved. [P42-P49, P52-P58]

### 5.2 Unit of analysis and transfer coding

The unit is a particular claim and its subsequent evaluation or decision, possibly spanning multiple documents, conversations or scheduled runs. One document may contain several such episodes, while an hourly run may contain none. The nine papers and ten active owner streams remain nested within **one organizational case**; they are not independent experimental participants.

For each episode we record who commissioned the work, the original claim and source version, where it was retained, whether a named recipient demonstrably encountered, assessed or used it, and whether an authorized owner adopted, limited or declined it. These are **separate observable events**, not compulsory stages. Unknown or inaccessible records are identified rather than interpreted as negative outcomes. The complete event codebook, evidence grades and event-level rows appear in Supplement S5. [P17, P19, P42, P52-P54]

For this study, an individual human's claim-level ratification would require a dated, attributable communication approving the specific claim, version, evidence qualification and scope. Paper-owner scientific adoption instead requires an owner decision under the relevant branch policy. Commissioning an investigation, allowing a source upload or circulating a manuscript does not by itself ratify each scientific claim. These distinctions describe the available evidence rather than imposing a new governance rule on the original research. [P17, P19, P54]

### 5.3 Selection and assessment

The observed episodes were selected after the records were inspected because they expose consequential handoff or failure states. This purposive selection supports mechanism description and codebook development, not rates of success or failure. A future retrospective inventory must fix a time window, record all eligible episodes before assessing outcomes, and include favorable, adverse, unresolved, and mistaken-correction cases. Repeated copies of one source cannot enter the denominator as distinct confirmations. A cross-paper citation is not evidence of an independently validated result.

Each episode should retain a compact evidence packet with the original claim, source version, receiving decision, checks, counterarguments, and outcome. Coders should distinguish direct artifact inspection, source-reported results, same-code replay, independent numerical implementation, and external scientific assessment. Two independent coders should evaluate a frozen eligible set, report disagreement by field, adjudicate under a documented rule, and avoid seeing the author's preferred conclusion where feasible. Formal accuracy, numerical reproducibility, and physical interpretation require separate appropriate assessors. Incomplete historical execution packages remain unreplayed, regardless of the persuasiveness of their summaries.

### 5.4 Outcomes and failure conditions

Source inspection, participant reports, inferred exposure and unresolved access are recorded separately. A missing recipient record is not proof of non-receipt, while a source citation may show later use without proving exactly how the recipient obtained the material. Memory and project-file sharing can bypass repository records. The complete coding conventions and examples are in Supplement S5. [P52-P54]

An observed correction establishes that a concern was identified and addressed in a specific record; it does not measure error-detection recall without a known pool of errors. A transfer safeguard is challenged by a documented loss of source provenance, unqualified promotion of a speculative claim, or unjustified rejection of a supported result. A stopped experiment can represent correct methodological restraint, not failure. Across-year comparisons are confounded by changing models, tasks, prompt practices, and author experience. Favorable results are never required for episode inclusion.

Four concern November coordination: software replay (M-01), source reuse with a retained STOP (M-02), reported wrong-chat routing (M-03), and Paper II non-adoption (U-01). U-03 is an earlier scientific-review illustration; U-02 concerns later commentary on this methods manuscript and is retained only in the supplement. These purposive cases are not randomized or representative. Table 3 summarizes the operational cases and illustration in plain language; Supplement S5 preserves all six event traces. [P17-P23, P31-P33, P42-P58]

The event codebook distinguishes an author commissioning work, a source being deposited, a recipient making an assessment, subsequent reliance on a source and authoritative scientific adoption. A revised document does not alone prove which criticism caused the edit; similarly, a successful scheduled run or repository commit does not prove that a scientific owner saw the output. The pilot was self-coded by the AI coordinator, and no second-coder reliability estimate exists. This is a division of responsibilities, not evidence that separate conversations automatically produced independent scientific results. [P14-P17, P20, P24-P30]

The MAIN owner later identified and examined that source at MAIN:P2-426, retained an identifiability obstacle, and withheld adoption. The owner decision reports mathematical checks, but the methods coordinator did not rerun those original computations. This example illustrates the difference between an additional research conversation generating a candidate and the paper owner making an authoritative scientific decision. It does not validate the proposed perceptual mechanism. [P21, P36]

It inspected one candidate file at an immutable source head for each paper; these are document types and provisional status markers, not nine successful discoveries. [P34, P38]

### 6.2 Shared Source Intelligence across more than one paper

The source-research role had a **cross-paper** remit from the November 15 governance documents: locate original studies, methods, data, code and contradictory evidence; retain a source-owned dossier; and submit reviewable material without automatically modifying the scientific paper. John authorized an exceptional November 17 delivery of additive R4 packets onto ten owner branches. The authorization and repository deposits are distinguishable from owner scientific adoption. [P16-P19]

The pre-review handoff documents an example outside Paper II. For Paper VII it reports source material on cultural transmission, minority-driven convention changes and conventional historical-persistence mechanisms. It traces an original-data dossier to a Paper VII owner examination and a later R4 dossier to Paper VII A55/A56 follow-on work. The source and owner records were not fully replayed as part of this manuscript, so the case is evidence of a **reported, traceable review chain**, not an independent reproduction of the original social-science analyses. The key methodological outcome was that conventional social-learning accounts remained relevant rivals; the source material was not promoted into proof of an extraordinary collective mechanism. [P16, P34, P37]

A more directly inspected Paper II chain provides a second example: the SI-R4 visual-completion dossier was committed onto the Paper II MAIN branch and explicitly cited by MAIN:P2-465 for bounded source analysis. MAIN:P2-464's separate STOP on a distinctive physical H/A claim remained in force. One source packet was used; a different proposed scientific inference was still blocked. The reviewer must not collapse those facts into either wholesale acceptance or a failed transfer. [P19, P22, P32, P36]

### 6.3 Scheduled research and repository continuity are separate systems

John requested recurring research in originating conversations. A November 17 read-only scheduler inspection found ten enabled hourly paper-owner tasks (one for each of Papers I and III–IX, and two for Paper II). At the later snapshot, nine selected latest runs were complete and one newer MAIN run was in progress. Twelve examined task-detail records had an unknown delivery status, including disabled support/legacy records. The study therefore verifies an hourly **configuration and selected retained outputs**, not a full execution history, uninterrupted research, correct destination delivery, or a scientific completion rate. [P15-P16, P20, P37]

The intended continuity mechanism was repeatable reading and writing of stable branch heads, prior decisions, unresolved questions, source dossiers and issue reservations. GitHub retained artifacts across conversations but could not force a scheduled message into the correct chat. A November 15 checkpoint documented a correct repository destination alongside a different conversation destination; a legacy task report also described wrong-chat delivery despite a completed scheduler record. These operational observations are consistent with the author’s stated concern about keeping scheduled work in its originating chat; they do not independently establish the motive for every intervention. [P16-P17, P20, P23, P37]

The permanent-branch policy formalized one owner branch per originating research stream rather than a new branch per hourly investigation. Two equal-duration PR-creation searches around the November 15 policy merge returned 686 before and one afterward, but the policy deliberately changed the PR transaction mechanism; the numbers do **not** measure fewer scientific tasks, less human work or greater accuracy. Direct owner-branch pushes also did not automatically trigger the documented GitHub Actions workflow, so reduced PR creation cannot be interpreted as improved auditability. [P10, P17, P23, P41]

### 6.4 Corrections, falsification-first limits and mathematics specialization

One inspected CI sequence (draft PR 2041) failed one of nine tests because archived output contained Python version 3.13.5 while the replay environment identified 3.14.4. A narrowly revised test comparison normalized the provenance field, and the later job passed, reporting six exact example checks. The record supports a software-replay correction; it is not a mathematical reproof, physical observation or owner adoption. Preserving that distinction is part of the research discipline. [P29, P31]

The November 15 falsification-first protocol addressed the author's concern that repeated mathematical or simulated investigations might not advance the fundamental testable questions. It required researchers to identify conventional rivals, distinguish model consequences from physical evidence and pivot when mathematics-only progress ceased to change a scientific decision. On November 19 John added a **separate cross-paper mathematics specialist** to review derivations, mathematical prior art and proposed formulas and to return candidate findings to the paper owners. That role did **not** exist during the earlier November 15 registry or September UQCP reviews. Its creation is documented as an author response to a research-management problem; improved scientific productivity has not been demonstrated. [P9-P10, P16-P17, S-GOV-01]

### 6.5 A separate AI reviewing service: earlier UQCP example

The author reports a practice of sending scientific drafts to an AI service separate from the primary drafting conversations, then relaying criticism and revised drafts between systems. One earlier example concerns four successive UQCP versions marked September 2026. In the supplied PDFs, v6 separately lists candidate M2 and M3 mechanisms, v7 joins their transport/crosstalk treatment and adds a channel-length constraint, v8 narrows the biological mechanism and adds per-control gates, and v9 distinguishes three transport components and adds a quasi-static drive null. The source PDFs document those textual changes; a later participant-regenerated account reports the reviewing comments but does not prove their original message bytes or that a particular criticism caused an edit. [P42-P48, P55-P58]

This case represents **scientific criticism outside the primary writing role**—one support channel in the research method. It predates the nine-paper GitHub organization and cannot demonstrate hourly coordination or computational independence. It also does not amount to four independent reviewing agents. A detailed 46-item coding of the reconstructed review messages was developed *later to study the process*; the self-coded counts and version crosswalk are retained in the supplement, not treated as a central finding about nine-paper research performance. [P44-P49, P52, P55-P58]

### 6.6 Human direction, governance corrections and subsequent self-study

The author's documented or reported interventions include dividing the research into distinct papers; emphasizing Paper II; creating a coordinator and source role; requesting fixed branch ownership and correct originating-chat delivery; authorizing a specific source-packet delivery exception; redirecting repetitive mathematics toward more discriminating work; and correcting this methods paper when it began to study the underlying physics instead of the workflow. These are documented or author-reported instances of strategic steering, not a measured intervention rate or evidence that the author personally ratified each specific scientific claim. [P14-P20, P28-P30, P40, S-GOV-01]

The later methodology project used historian conversations in the original Postulations, Duplicates/Dynamic Door and Postulations2 settings to gather evidence and used a coordinator to synthesize it. These were **documentation roles created to study the earlier organization**, not proof that this historical research apparatus operated throughout the original scientific program. The same separate AI service later reviewed versions of the present methods paper and corrected some of its own retrospective descriptions. Because that reviewer had participated in the studied scientific reviews, those later comments are participant fact-checking rather than independent methods peer review. [P28-P30, P42-P58]

### 6.7 What the records can and cannot establish

Across the documented cases, repository deposit, source notice, recipient reliance, scientific assessment, selected CI success and manuscript adoption are distinct actions. A source packet can inform another paper while an experimental claim remains blocked; an hourly run can complete while destination delivery is unknown; a mathematical candidate can be examined and explicitly not adopted; and a separate reviewer may raise criticism without proving a subsequent change was caused by it. These are descriptive findings about **research organization and evidence discipline**, not proof of higher scientific quality. [P17-P23, P31-P41, P54]

The project evidence remains concentrated in selected November repository episodes and one earlier UQCP review chain. Complete original conversation exports, model histories, systematic human work-time records and an independent second coding are not available. A later participant attempted an access test of fifteen sampled documentary claims: four accessible UQCP text/file facts were reported confirmed and eleven were inaccessible or missing to that participant. That exercise documents permission limitations, not independent reliability or the success rate of the nine-paper organization. Its scoring sheets, detailed event log and critique inventory remain supplemental. [P20, P41-P58; Supplement S5-S8]

## 7 Future evaluation, not an observed result

The original November 17 methods draft already proposed an independently scored comparison before the second AI reviewed this paper. That was an **evaluation proposal**, not an operational practice or measured outcome. The later reviews refined its endpoint and controls. A fair prospective test would compare structured, source-versioned handoffs with plain-text handoffs while giving both arms the same two-stage session boundaries, sources, scripted operator, model-call budget and opportunities for critical review. [P16, P33, P51-P54]

The primary endpoint would be whether the recipient's arm-neutral decision memo correctly preserves a seeded claim's evidentiary status, source version and essential qualification. Missing components count as failures, and unsupported-claim acceptance requires a separate safety guard. Participants and coders would be independent of the present case study where feasible; task-family clustering, isolation of shared memory, blinding checks and full preregistration would be necessary. **This experiment has not been performed.** The full proposed protocol is preserved as Supplement S9 rather than presented as a principal finding of the retrospective study.

## 8 Discussion and limitations

**RQ1—Organization.** The case documents a human-initiated division of work among nine distinct scientific questions, with extra capacity allocated to Paper II and shared coordination and source-research roles. The additional matrix of nine provisional candidates shows activity across the entire program, not scientific confirmation or equal productivity. The reviewing service was reportedly used before and during the coordinated period; the supplied UQCP example predates the November repository structure. The mathematics specialist is a later, untested role change. [P14-P20, P34, P38, P42-P48, S-GOV-01]

**RQ2—Research exchange.** The selected records establish that a source packet can be used without lifting a scientific restriction, and that an additional paper-owner stream can offer a candidate which the authoritative owner considers but does not adopt. The reported Paper VII source-to-owner sequence gives evidence of cross-paper support, with incomplete original replay. Cross-AI manuscript criticism supplies a different review channel; similar wording across successive versions does not prove that the reviewer caused each revision. [P16-P23, P31-P41, P44-P58]

**RQ3—Operational continuity.** Ten scheduled paper-owner tasks and persistent branches made recurrent work and retained results possible. A completed task, branch commit or passing software check did not necessarily certify intended-chat delivery, validation or adoption. Governance corrections were documented, but neither policy adoption nor changing PR creation counts establishes increased research quality. [P10, P15-P20, P23, P37, P40-P41]

The study is a reflexive, selectively preserved case rather than a census. Its coordinator produced much of the narrative and self-coded the six selected episodes and the 46 UQCP critique items. Original early conversations, some execution packages, model configurations and source data remain incomplete; reviewer messages for the earlier UQCP example were regenerated from participant context. Similar results across AI streams may reflect shared prompts, sources or platform memory rather than independent corroboration. A participant later attempted to score 15 retained documentary assertions, but could access only four already familiar UQCP document claims; that is an access-feasibility finding, not an independent reliability audit. The full event log and this access-test record are preserved in Supplement S5–S7. [P24-P30, P35-P40, P44-P58]

Independent outside verification remains constrained by private GitHub files and conversation access. Author-approved restricted access or a redacted, hash-linked evidence packet is needed for material repository claims. The present working copy also intentionally omits AI service and model identities; a complete venue-specific disclosure and human-responsibility declaration must precede publication. These limitations do not turn missing evidence into a negative finding, nor do they substantiate the nine proposed physical or consciousness mechanisms. [P41, P49-P58]

## 9 Conclusion

The Postulations case documents an evolving, human-initiated research organization rather than a new autonomous research agent: nine paper scopes, concentrated multi-chat work on Paper II, shared coordination and source research, recurring paper-owner runs, GitHub persistence, a separate scientific-review channel and a later mathematics specialist. The most defensible observed outcome is not a count of generated papers or reviewer criticisms, but the practical need to distinguish roles, source transfers, scientific decisions and human interventions. Selected records preserve useful restrictions, non-adoptions, software corrections and routing problems without showing that the system is more productive or scientifically accurate than alternatives. The underlying nine scientific hypotheses remain outside this methods study. The claim-transfer coding and prospective comparison remain supporting tools for future independent evaluation.

## 10 Contributions, availability and next steps

The contribution is a retrospective organizational history, grounded in pre-review workflow records and selected later source checks, of a human researcher coordinating multiple AI research functions. The four November repository and scheduling episodes, the earlier UQCP scientific-review example and subsequent participant feedback on this study have **different dates and evidentiary roles**. The event codebook, six-case self-coding, 46-item UQCP classification, participant access test and prospective comparison are preserved as supplementary methods, not historical requirements of the original nine-paper organization.

The next publication tasks are editorial and evidentiary rather than another broad science investigation: select a case-study or experience-report outlet; prepare its required AI-use and human-responsibility disclosure; provide appropriate controlled source access; and seek an uninvolved evidence checker for material claims. Any future experimental comparison of research architectures should be a separately preregistered study. No research schedules, science papers or owner decisions were changed in this manuscript revision.
