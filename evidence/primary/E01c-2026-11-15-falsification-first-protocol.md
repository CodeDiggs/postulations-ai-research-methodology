# Falsification-first research protocol — v1 (2026-11-15)

**Status:** User-directed research workflow and prioritization, not scientific acceptance of any theory, equation, result, manuscript, or experimental authorization. Applicable to every originating paper research stream, including distinct Paper II MAIN, IND, Postulations2 and legacy identities. Read this at the start of **every iteration**, after current main AGENTS.md and the appropriate seven-part paper INTERFACE; also read [paper-specific decision roadmaps](paper-decision-roadmaps-v1.md).

## Purpose

The purpose of the nine-paper program is **not** to accumulate true mathematical statements indefinitely. It is to discover which specific claims about consciousness, access, recursion, memory, collective influence, informational foundations and physical events are mathematically coherent, independently testable and supported or contradicted by evidence. Every proposed step must either change a falsifiable scientific decision or remove an independently identified obstacle to making such a decision.

**Mathematical proof can establish a consequence within precisely defined assumptions or a rigorous impossibility/identifiability result. It cannot by itself prove the physical existence of a consciousness field, actual infinite experience, nonbiological memory or perception-caused source events.** A failure to distinguish models is not proof either model is false. Rejection of one conventional rival does not prove the postulation.

## Required per-iteration decision card (before new calculations)

1. **Central paper-specific claim H:** Write a one-sentence narrow operational claim, and state the stronger theory claim it does *not* entail. Do not let a local nuisance-parameter theorem become the central claim.
2. **Decisive contrast:** Identify the **strongest plausible ordinary rival H0**, its matching information and resources, an independently measurable observable O, and the interventions/conditions under which H and H0 make different *quantitative* predictions. If they are observationally equivalent under all defensible conditions, say **NOT IDENTIFIABLE** and do not keep tuning the same mathematical construction.
3. **Decision boundary:** State beforehand what observation/result would **support the restricted model relative to H0**, what would **falsify/reject that restricted model under its assumptions**, and what would remain **inconclusive**. A universal/speculative ontology cannot be declared globally proven or disproven by one finite test.
4. **Minimum needed mathematics:** Identify the smallest theorem, bound, sample-size calculation, code check, source recovery, or instrument calibration that would make the decisive contrast testable. Give the exact decision that would change if this work succeeds or fails.
5. **Feasibility:** List missing actual data/instrument, independent calibration, historical source, ethics/consent, cost, resolution, or sample-size prerequisites. Never imply a simulated instrument or synthetic human record exists.
6. **Novelty check:** Compare this planned step with the last two **source-owned** iterations and prior art. If the new calculation changes no distinguishable prediction, no falsification boundary, no needed assumption, no test feasibility, and no independent verification status, **do not launch it**. Record the line as parked/no-decision progress instead of inventing another experiment number.

## Research priority order (choose the highest feasible)

1. **Direct discriminating empirical/design milestone:** registered observation or intervention with genuine source truth, information-matched rival models, negative controls, and a realistic ethics/resources gate.
2. **Critical experimental prerequisite:** validated measurement, calibration transport, real-world data acquisition plan, effect-size/power or source-provenance requirement whose outcome changes the decisive test.
3. **Independent verification of decisive prior claim:** recovered original data/code, independent implementation, adversarial model fit, proof audit or negative result that could reverse a planned decision.
4. **Necessary mathematical proposition:** only if explicitly linked to the discriminating observation or proof/no-go result. Prioritize a crisp theorem and stop when sufficient.
5. **Exploratory mathematical extension:** allowed when it offers a concrete new prediction or empirically relevant constraint; otherwise park as a low-priority appendix, not the next hourly PR.

Two consecutive math/simulation-only runs without a meaningful new experimental decision trigger **a mandatory pivot** in the following iteration: source recovery, an actual validation protocol, independent adversarial review, a minimum feasible test, or an explicit **BLOCKED / NON-IDENTIFIABLE / NO NEW DECISION** report. The goal is not compulsory positive findings or a fixed number of experiments.

## Per-run deliverable, even when no experiment runs

At beginning or in the Issue, write the decision card; at end, record:

- **Claim under test** and **ordinary rival**, restricted assumptions and the exact observable/contrast.
- **Decision updated?** Yes/no; if yes, precisely which threshold/model/protocol/conclusion changed; if no, explain why and park repetitive work.
- **Current evidence tier:** theorem, deterministic/synthetic check, historical same-code replay, independently implemented check, actual human/animal/physical data, or peer review. Maintain these separately from scientific/manuscript adoption.
- **Most decisive next step:** a specific experiment, independent source-recovery task, feasible instrument-validation action, or explanation why the claim is not currently testable. Identify the external blocker and any necessary approvals.
- **Stop/failure rules:** resource caps, failed CI/archive, inadequate identifiability, out-of-class ordinary rival, absent measurement or recruitment/ethics prohibition. Preserve failures and contradictory results.

Use the seven-part candidate paper structure, pre-register new substantive experiments before calculating, keep original IDs and source chats separate, submit changes through reviewable branches/PRs, and keep accepted research/INTERFACE/STATUS unchanged unless separately reviewed. Passing CI is not peer review.

## Avoid the theorem treadmill

- Do not spend a third iteration refining a bound on a bound unless it materially changes the **hypothesis decision, physical test feasibility, or model-discriminating prediction**.
- A mathematically valid no-go theorem counts as progress **once it closes an unidentifiable route**; follow it by testing a distinct operational route, explicitly narrowing the theory or marking the strong claim untestable. Do not endlessly generate more isomorphic counterexamples.
- Do not mistake large simulated grids, experiment numbering, many passed tests, or similar results across different papers for confirmation of the core principle.
- Give priority to a **scientifically valuable negative conclusion** over nominally positive synthetic results with unmeasured assumptions.
- No study with humans/animals, physical apparatus changes, spending or experiment recruitment is authorized by this protocol.

## Plain-language checkpoint question

End **every research iteration** by answering, without mathematics jargon: **“What would we actually have to observe to make this paper's claim more believable or less believable, and did this run bring us meaningfully closer to that observation?”** A permissible answer is **“No; this direction is blocked or redundant.”** Keep source-owned delivery in that source conversation; a GitHub comment does not prove it was delivered to another chat.
